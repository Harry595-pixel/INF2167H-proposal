Data Cleaning

  • Cleaned variable names using janitor package (Firke 2023)

  • Created a new table with only necessary variables

    • Object ID: unique ID of tree

    • Ward: tree location by geographical district

    • Botanical name: formal scientific name of tree

    • DBH: diameter of tree’s trunk; indicative of tree’s maturity

Data Cleaning (Continued)

  • Derived new variables based on existing variables:

    • Region based on ward (e.g., Wards 6, 8, 15, 16, 17, and 18 are considered North York)

    • Species, genus, and family based on botanical name using classification by WorldFlora package (Kindt 2020)

Linear Regression Model (pt.4)

Findings

  • Wards that severely breach the 20% genus threshold rely heavily on larger, mature trees

  • This reflects the historical urban forestry practice of over-planting of Acer (Maples).

Findings

  • Other genus types have few to no mature trees due to lower survival rates, resulting from harsh weather and road conditions

  • Over time, more Acer trees were likely planted because of their known resilience

Findings

  • Exceeding the 30% family threshold is associated with significantly smaller average trunk diameters

  • Acer genus is under the Sapindaceae family

    • How can there be lower average DBH for when the Sapindaceae population increases but higher average DBH for when the Acer family increases?

Findings

  • Recent mass planting of Sapindaceae trees that are NOT under Acer genus

  • Efforts are being made to plant trees outside of the Acer genus, but the 10-20-30 rule is still being broken at the family-level

Findings

  • Prediction: Arborists see the potential dangers of planting too many trees of the same type, but they also want to plant trees that have higher survival rates.

  • As a result, they plant trees that are adjacent to Acer genus in hopes of having more diversity but similar survival rates.
